Navantia USV

Project Overview

Design and development of a 3D printing strategy for producing a large-scale USV (Unmanned Surface Vehicle) model using Navantia's industrial 7×3m 3D printing system.

Engineering Challenge

The brief required sectioning a full-size USV hull into printable segments that fit within three 7×3m print beds, while maintaining structural integrity and assembly precision. The approach proposed hydrofoil integration — adding retractable T-shaped foils to the vessel hull.

3D Printing Strategy

The hull was divided into 6 main sections: Popa (stern), Centro (mid-section), Centro Motor (engine zone), Proa (bow), Antena (antenna), and Tapa Motor (motor cover). Each section was oriented vertically for optimal print strength.

Joining strategy used anchor points at the hydrofoils and upper hull seams, bonded with resin and structural adhesive. The antenna is attached with high-strength adhesive.

Hydrofoil Addition

Inspired by the Candela C-8, T-shaped hydrofoils were integrated into the design proposal, positioned based on reference data from a 7.7m Candela C-7 hydroboat (2400mm beam, 7700mm LOA). This proposed capability to evaluate hydrofoil integration for future USV development.

Deliverables

Complete sectioning plan across 3 print beds, structural joint design, reference engineering data, 3D assembly documentation, and presentation panels for Navantia's engineering team.
